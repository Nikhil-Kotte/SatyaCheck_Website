# Marketing Suite

One skill, 25 modes. Each mode has an **Intake** (ask before producing), an **Output** spec, and **Rules**.

## How to run

1. **Pick the mode.** Match the request to a mode in the index. If the user names a mode, use it. If two modes fit, pick the closer one and say which you picked in one line. If nothing fits clearly, ask.
2. **Run intake.** Ask the intake questions for that mode in a single message, numbered. Skip any the user already answered. If the user says "just go" or supplies partial info, proceed and list your assumptions at the top of the output.
3. **Produce the output** in the exact section order of the mode's Output spec, using markdown headings per section.
4. **Apply the global rules** below to every mode.
5. **Offer one next step** at the end (for example, "Want me to run Hook Creator on the winning angle?"). One line only.

## Global rules (apply to every mode)

**Honesty and data**
- Never invent statistics, benchmarks, testimonials, customer quotes, review counts, or case-study results. If proof is needed and the user has not supplied it, insert a clearly marked placeholder like `[INSERT REAL STAT]` or `[CUSTOMER QUOTE NEEDED]`.
- Keyword volume, keyword difficulty, CPC, Quality Score, and "expected impact" are **estimates** unless the user supplies tool data (Ahrefs, Semrush, Google Ads, GA4, etc.). Label them as estimates (Low / Medium / High) rather than precise numbers.
- Scores (SEO, AEO, CRO 1 to 10) are qualitative judgments. Always show the breakdown that produced the score.
- If a URL is given and you cannot fetch it, say so and ask the user to paste the content. Do not guess page contents.
- Do not claim knowledge of current platform algorithms, optimal post times, or ad policies as fact. Frame them as common practice and recommend the user verify against their own analytics.

**Writing quality**
- Specific over generic. Every line should only make sense for this product and audience.
- Write to one specific reader, in plain human language.
- Banned words and openers: "game-changer", "revolutionary", "unlock", "unleash", "elevate", "in today's fast-paced world", "look no further", "take it to the next level".
- One primary CTA per asset unless the mode says otherwise.
- Vary angles across channels and assets. Never reuse the same opener twice in one output.
- Match the brand voice given in intake. If none is given, default to clear, direct, conversational.

**Format**
- Use tables where the Output spec asks for them. Otherwise headings plus short paragraphs or bullets.
- Keep character or word limits stated in each mode. State the count when a limit matters (for example, subject lines, meta descriptions).

## Category 1: Content creation

### 1. Hook Creator
Use for: email subject lines, social openers, ad headlines, landing page headlines, video titles.

**Intake**
1. Content type (email, social, ad, landing page, video)
2. Target audience
3. Main benefit or promise
4. Tone (professional, casual, urgent, playful)

**Output**
- 10 hooks, each prefixed with its framework in brackets, e.g. `[Curiosity Gap] ...`
- Use at least 5 different frameworks from: Curiosity Gap, Pain-Agitation-Solution, Benefit-Driven, Contrarian, Specific Numbers, Question + Benefit, Social Proof, Urgency/FOMO, Transformation, Listicle.
- After the list, one line naming the 2 to 3 you would test first and why.

**Rules**
- Under 100 characters where possible.
- At least 2 hooks must challenge category norms.
- Specific Numbers and Social Proof hooks must use numbers or proof the user supplied, or a `[placeholder]`.

### 2. Video Script Generator
Use for: TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts, long-form YouTube, 15 seconds to 10 minutes.

**Intake**
1. Platform (TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts, YouTube long-form)
2. Video length
3. Topic or message
4. Goal (educate, entertain, sell, build awareness)
5. Brand voice
6. Hook style (pattern interrupt, curiosity gap, bold statement)

**Output: short-form (under 90 seconds)**
- Hook (0 to 3s): pattern interrupt
- Value promise (3 to 5s)
- Main content with on-screen text callouts
- CTA (last 5s)

**Output: long-form (90 seconds and above)**
- Hook, intro, 3 to 5 main sections each opening with a mini-hook, recap, CTA

**Both formats include:** timestamps, spoken script, on-screen text, shot suggestions, music cues, caption copy, thumbnail concept (for YouTube).

**Rules**
- One CTA only.
- Script length must fit the stated duration (rough guide: about 2 to 2.5 spoken words per second; state this as approximate).

### 3. Blog Post Writer
Use for: SEO content intended to rank.

**Intake**
1. Topic and target keyword
2. Search intent (informational, commercial, navigational)
3. Target audience and their pain points
4. Desired word count
5. Competing articles to beat (URLs)
6. Internal links to include
7. CTA at the end

**Output**
- Meta title (about 60 characters or less) and meta description (about 155 characters or less)
- H1
- Hook intro
- 4 to 6 H2 sections
- FAQ (3 to 5 questions)
- Conclusion with CTA
- Image suggestions (placement plus description)
- Internal link placements (anchor text plus target)

**Rules**
- Each section answers the reader's next question before they ask it.
- Never open with "In today's fast-paced world" or similar.
- If competitor URLs cannot be read, say so and ask for pasted content or proceed without competitor comparison.

### 4. Content Repurposing Engine
Use for: turning any long-form piece (blog, guide, research, podcast) into multi-platform assets.

**Intake**
1. Original content (paste or link)
2. Platforms to repurpose for
3. Brand voice
4. Primary CTA for each asset
5. Audience per platform (may differ)

**Output (only for requested platforms)**
- LinkedIn: insight-driven hook, key takeaways, CTA
- Twitter/X: 6 to 8 tweet thread, strong opener, clear progression
- Instagram: carousel concept with slide-by-slide copy
- Newsletter: 120 to 150 words, subject line, CTA
- YouTube: outline with hook, 3 to 5 sections, CTA
- TikTok: 60-second script with pattern-interrupt opening
- Lead magnet: outline for a downloadable resource

**Rules**
- Same core insight, different framing per channel.
- No two assets share an opener.

### 5. Podcast Content Repurposer
Use for: turning an episode transcript into a multi-channel content system.

**Intake**
1. Transcript or episode summary
2. Episode title and guest bio (if applicable)
3. Key listener takeaways
4. Target audience and tone
5. Distribution channels

**Output**
- SEO show notes: timestamps, takeaways, resources mentioned, metadata (title, description, tags)
- Platform posts: LinkedIn, Instagram caption plus story frames, Twitter/X thread, TikTok script
- Email section with 3 subject line options
- 5 pull quotes, each under 20 words
- 3 clip recommendations with timestamps and suggested captions
- Standalone blog post: SEO title, 3 to 5 sections, CTA to listen

**Rules**
- Pull quotes must be verbatim from the transcript. If only a summary is given, label quotes as paraphrased and do not attribute exact wording to the guest.
- Timestamps only from the transcript. If none exist, write `[timestamp]`.
- Vary angles across channels.

### 6. Social Media Content Calendar
Use for: a 30-day content plan across LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok, X, Facebook.

**Intake**
1. Platforms and posting frequency
2. Business and brand info
3. Content pillars (3 to 5 themes)
4. Campaigns or launches to build around
5. Brand voice and primary goal

**Output**
- Table: `Date | Platform | Content Type | Post Concept | Full Caption | Hashtags | Post Time | Visual Format | CTA | Notes`
- Content mix check at the end showing actual percentages vs target
- Cross-platform adaptation notes
- Batch creation guidance (what to produce together in one session)

**Rules**
- Target mix: 40% educational, 30% engagement, 20% promotional, 10% behind-the-scenes.
- Post times are suggested starting points, labeled as such; recommend checking the account's own analytics.
- If the full month would be too long for one response, deliver in weekly chunks and ask before continuing.

## Category 2: Email and lifecycle

### 7. Email Sequence Builder
Use for: welcome, nurture, launch, and re-engagement flows of 3 to 7 emails.

**Intake**
1. Sequence goal and type
2. Audience segment and number of emails (3 to 7)
3. Timing cadence and brand voice
4. Key value propositions and existing offers

**Output (per email)**
- Send timing and purpose
- 3 subject line options plus preview text
- Full body: hook, content, CTA
- Design notes
- Success metric

**Rules**
- Each email must stand alone (reader may not have opened the previous one).
- Urgency in at most 1 email of the sequence.
- Vary length and format across the sequence.

### 8. Newsletter Writer
Use for: a complete newsletter issue from a topic or brief.

**Intake**
1. Main topic and secondary story or link
2. Audience, tone, and sponsor placement (if any)
3. CTA for this issue

**Output**
- 3 subject line options and preview text
- Opening hook (1 to 2 sentences)
- Main section (400 to 600 words)
- Secondary story or curated picks
- Sponsor slot (if requested)
- Closing CTA
- Estimated read time

**Rules**
- Write like a person, not a brand.
- Curated picks only from links the user provides. Do not invent URLs or resources.

### 9. Re-engagement Campaign Builder
Use for: win-back of cold subscribers or lapsed customers.

**Intake**
1. Audience segment and length of inactivity
2. Last meaningful interaction and best reason to re-engage
3. Brand voice

**Output: 3 emails, each with subject line, preview text, full body, timing**
- Email 1: acknowledge absence without guilt, offer real value
- Email 2: social proof or what they missed, clear benefit
- Email 3: honest sunset email, low-pressure farewell, final CTA (for example, "stay subscribed" button)

**Rules**
- Personal, not desperate. No guilt-tripping.

## Category 3: SEO and AEO

### 10. SEO Content Optimizer
Use for: auditing existing content for search and AI-citation performance.

**Intake**
1. Content to analyze (paste or URL)
2. Target keyword and audience
3. Content goal and top competing URLs

**Output**
- SEO score 1 to 10 with breakdown (keyword targeting, structure, depth, readability, internal linking, metadata)
- Top 3 structural weaknesses
- 5 specific changes to make immediately
- Missing entities, semantic gaps, subtopics to add
- AEO section: question-answer pairs and heading structures that help AI models cite the piece
- One rewritten section as an example

### 11. AEO Content Optimizer
Use for: making content more likely to be cited by AI search (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini).

**Intake**
1. Content to analyze (paste or URL)
2. Primary query this should answer
3. Target audience and content format

**Output**
- AEO score 1 to 10 with breakdown
- Structural weaknesses for AI parsing
- 5 immediate fixes
- Question-answer pairs to add
- Entity-based language improvements
- One rewritten section optimized for direct citation

**Optimize for:** descriptive headings, Q&A pairs, direct authoritative answers in the first sentence of a section, specific data with named sources, entity-based language, topical comprehensiveness.

**Rules**
- AEO is an emerging practice; frame recommendations as current best practice, not guaranteed outcomes.
- "Specific data with sources" means sources the user can verify. Use placeholders if the user has none.

### 12. Site Architecture Advisor
Use for: website content structure, topic clusters, internal linking.

**Intake**
1. Website URL or sitemap, and primary topics
2. Top 5 pages by traffic and business goals
3. Existing pillar pages, if any

**Output**
- Current architecture diagnosis
- Topic cluster recommendations (pillar plus supporting pages)
- Internal linking map (table: From page | To page | Anchor text)
- Pages to consolidate or expand
- Missing content per cluster
- Priority fix order with expected impact (High / Medium / Low, labeled as estimate)

### 13. Keyword Research Strategist
Use for: a prioritized keyword plan for a topic area or product category.

**Intake**
1. Topic area or product category
2. Target audience and business stage
3. Competing sites to benchmark against
4. Content types I can produce
5. (Optional) any keyword tool export (Ahrefs, Semrush, GSC) to ground the numbers

**Output**
- Primary keywords with intent and estimated difficulty
- Long-tail variations and question-based queries
- Semantic clusters
- Content angle per cluster
- 30-day content calendar suggestion

**Rules**
- Without tool data, difficulty and volume are Low / Medium / High estimates only. Never output fake exact search volumes.
- Recommend validating in a keyword tool before committing.

## Category 4: Paid advertising

### 14. Ad Creative Refresher
Use for: diagnosing creative fatigue and generating new hooks and angles.

**Intake**
1. Platform, current creative, and performance metrics (CTR, CPA, frequency, trend over time)
2. Offer, target audience, and what has stopped working

**Output**
- Fatigue signals diagnosis (based on supplied metrics)
- Hook matrix: 20+ concepts across pain-focused, aspiration-focused, social proof, urgency, contrarian (table: Angle | Hook | Visual idea)
- Top 5 hooks to test first with rationale
- A/B test structure with success metrics
- Platform-specific adaptations and format variations

**Rules**
- Every concept must be specific to the offer.

### 15. Paid Ads Auditor
Use for: structured audit of a Google Ads or Meta account.

**Intake**
1. Platform and account performance data (CSV export or key metrics)
2. Business goal, budget allocation, and top campaigns

**Output**
- Wasted spend: campaigns, ad sets, or search terms with spend and zero conversions
- Quality Score or relevance issues
- Audience overlap and creative fatigue signals
- Bid strategy mismatches
- Prioritized fix list (table: Fix | Impact | Effort)
- Quick wins implementable in under 1 hour

**Rules**
- Findings must come from the supplied data. If a check needs data that was not provided, list it under "Data needed" instead of guessing.
- If a CSV is provided, analyze it programmatically where possible and show the figures used.

### 16. A/B Test Setup Generator
Use for: designing statistically sound marketing experiments.

**Intake**
1. What I want to test and current baseline metric
2. Minimum improvement needed before acting (minimum detectable effect)
3. Audience size or traffic available per day, and platform

**Output**
- Specific, falsifiable hypothesis
- Control and variant definitions
- Sample size per variant (state assumptions: significance level, power, one- or two-sided)
- Recommended duration (sample size divided by daily traffic, rounded up to full weeks)
- Primary and guardrail success metrics
- Common mistakes to avoid for this test type

**Rules**
- Compute sample size with a standard two-proportion formula (default alpha 0.05 two-sided, power 0.80) and show the inputs. If code execution is available, calculate rather than estimate.
- If traffic is too low to detect the requested effect in a reasonable time, say so plainly and suggest alternatives (larger effect, different metric, qualitative testing).

## Category 5: CRO and copy

### 17. Landing Page CRO Reviewer
Use for: auditing a landing page for conversion.

**Intake**
1. Landing page URL or pasted content (screenshots welcome)
2. Goal, target audience, and traffic source
3. Current conversion rate, if known

**Output**
- CRO score 1 to 10 with category breakdown
- Above-the-fold diagnosis: headline, subhead, CTA, visual
- Value proposition and social proof assessment
- CTA analysis: copy, placement, friction (including form design)
- 5 specific changes ranked by expected impact
- Rewritten headline and CTA as examples

### 18. Copywriting Refresher
Use for: editing and improving existing marketing copy.

**Intake**
1. Copy to improve (paste the text)
2. Page type, target audience, goal, and brand voice

**Output**
- Diagnosis: what is weak and why (weak headlines, passive voice, vague benefits, buried CTAs, generic claims, features without outcomes)
- Full edited version with changes marked in [brackets]
- 3 headline alternatives and 3 CTA alternatives
- One-sentence summary of what improved and why

### 19. Homepage Copy Generator
Use for: conversion-focused homepage copy from scratch.

**Intake**
1. Product description and target audience pain point
2. Top 3 benefits (not features)
3. Best social proof, primary CTA, and brand voice

**Output**
- Hero: headline under 10 words, subhead, CTA
- Value proposition block (3 benefits with brief copy)
- Feature-to-benefit section (outcomes, not functionality)
- Social proof block: testimonial format and stat callouts (user-supplied or placeholders)
- FAQ (3 to 5 questions, direct answers)
- Secondary CTA for hesitant visitors

**Rules**
- Write to one specific reader.

## Category 6: Research and positioning

### 20. Competitor Content Gap Analyzer
Use for: finding content opportunities competitors miss.

**Intake**
1. My website or business description
2. 3 to 5 competitor URLs and content goals
3. Content types I can produce and resources available

**Output**
1. Competitor overview table: Competitor | Strengths | Weaknesses | Apparent top topics | Content frequency
2. Prioritized gap table: Topic | Gap type (missing, shallow, format, unanswered question) | Difficulty | Impact | Unique angle | Format
3. Top 10 quick-win content ideas: Title | Reason | Difficulty | Keyword target
4. 3 to 5 long-term pillar opportunities

**Rules**
- Only analyze what you can actually read. If competitor sites cannot be fetched, say so and ask for sitemaps, blog indexes, or pasted content.
- "Top keywords" require tool data; without it, describe apparent focus topics instead.
- Prioritize by business results, not just traffic.

### 21. Customer Research Synthesizer
Use for: turning interviews, surveys, reviews, or support tickets into marketing insight.

**Intake**
1. Raw research (interviews, reviews, surveys, support tickets)
2. Product context and the marketing question to answer
3. Current messaging hypothesis

**Output**
- ICP summary: demographics, role, context, goals
- Top 3 pain points, each with supporting verbatim quotes
- Voice-of-customer language: exact phrases usable in copy
- Objection map: what stops them from buying
- Jobs-to-be-done insights
- Whether the data supports or contradicts the current messaging hypothesis
- Messaging recommendations and 3 copy angles to test

**Rules**
- Quotes must be verbatim from the supplied data. Note how many sources support each pain point so the user can judge strength.

### 22. Positioning and Messaging Strategist
Use for: positioning strategy and a messaging hierarchy.

**Intake**
1. Product description and target audience
2. Top 3 competitors and what differentiates me
3. Best customer outcomes and proof points

**Output**
- Category frame and target audience definition
- Differentiated value proposition (one sentence)
- Key messages per audience segment, each with proof points
- What to stop saying (positioning anti-patterns)
- Messaging architecture: primary, secondary, tertiary claims (table)

### 23. Problem Diagnostician
Use for: quick diagnosis of an underperforming channel (low opens, poor conversion, ad fatigue, falling engagement).

**Intake**
1. Problem description and current metrics
2. Channel, what I have already tried, and target audience

**Output**
- Root cause category: messaging, audience, timing, creative, or funnel
- Likely root cause (1 to 2 sentences) with confidence level and what evidence would confirm it
- 3 quick wins implementable in under 1 hour
- 2 strategic fixes for higher impact
- What to A/B test first and what success looks like in metrics

**Rules**
- Solutions must be specific to the stated channel and audience.
- Do not repeat things the user already tried.

## Category 7: Growth and strategy

### 24. Product Launch Playbook Generator
Use for: a full go-to-market launch plan.

**Intake**
1. Product being launched and target audience
2. Launch date, runway, channels, goal, and budget

**Output: phased plan**
- **Pre-launch:** teaser content, waitlist, press prep, partner outreach, internal enablement
- **Launch day:** announcement sequence, social posts, email to list, PR push
- **Post-launch:** follow-up content, case studies, ad creative, retargeting, testimonial collection

For each phase: specific tasks with dates relative to launch (for example, L-14), copy assets (drafted, not just named), channel sequence, success metric.

**Rules**
- Scale the plan to the stated budget and runway. Flag anything that does not fit.

### 25. Churn Prevention Playbook
Use for: a retention and recovery system, especially SaaS.

**Intake**
1. Product type, subscription model, and average customer lifetime
2. Known churn triggers, current cancellation flow, brand voice

**Output**
- **Early warning system:** churn signals at 30, 60, and 90 days with intervention triggers (table: Signal | Threshold | Trigger | Action)
- **Intervention messages by stage:**
  - Usage drop: re-engagement email with specific value reminder
  - Support friction: proactive outreach template
  - Pricing concern: response framework and offer options
  - Competitor evaluation: differentiation message
- **Cancellation flow:** what to say at each step to attempt recovery
- **Win-back campaign:** 3 emails for already-churned users, each with timing, angle, and offer

**Rules**
- Cancellation flow must stay easy to complete. No dark patterns (hidden cancel buttons, forced calls, guilt copy). Recovery offers are allowed; obstruction is not.
